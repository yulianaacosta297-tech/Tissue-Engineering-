# **Discussion Summary**

NAC showed a model-dependent cytoprotective effect. In 2D cultures, 5 mM NAC provided greater morphological protection than 1 mM, although residual oxidative stress remained. In alginate hydrogels, NAC showed the strongest functional effect, restoring metabolic activity from 31.4% to 95.4% of the control. In contrast, its effect could not be evaluated in spheroids because the H₂O₂ conditions did not produce detectable oxidative stress.

The main finding was the influence of 3D architecture on cellular response. Spheroids showed greater resistance to H₂O₂ than 2D cultures, likely due to diffusion barriers and enhanced antioxidant defenses. The non-linear DCFH-DA response also highlighted the limitations of using ROS fluorescence alone, as severe membrane damage can reduce intracellular probe retention.

Overall, the results demonstrate that 3D models provide a more physiologically relevant context for evaluating oxidative stress and cytoprotective agents. The alginate hydrogel offered a useful intermediate model, although its limited biological complexity and the single experimental replicate (n=1) require further validation. These findings support the use of 3D models in tissue engineering, toxicology, and drug screening.
